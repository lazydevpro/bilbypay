# Winners from Hackathons on Other Fast EVM Chains (plus Monad's Recent Winners), Mapped to the Monad Metropolis Tracks

Research date: 2026-09-24. Scope: 2024 to Sept 2026. Track key: **T1** = Onchain Finance & Trading, **T2** = Consumer Products & Payments, **T3** = Social, Attention & Culture, **T4** = Trust, Identity & AI Infrastructure.

Coverage note: I found verified winner lists for Sonic DeFAI, Hyperliquid Korea (HLH), Somnia Dreamathon, Somnia Reactivity, Base Onchain Summer 2024 (partial) and Monad evm/accathon (names only). Lists were partial or missing for Hyperliquid Community Hackathon (online), Sei ai/accelathon (grand prize only), Base Batches, Moltiverse, Monad Blitz, MegaETH, Berachain and Abstract. DoraHacks winner pages and X threads returned 403/405 errors or no text when fetched.

---

## Q1: Who won, and what did they build? (Structured list grouped by Metropolis track)

### Takeaway
Across the fast chains, most winners were **trading tools and AI agents that do trading** (T1). Sonic's 1st and 2nd place, Hyperliquid Korea's top 10, and many of Somnia's winners were yield allocators, copilots, copy-trading apps or risk tools. Consumer and payments winners (T2) mostly came from Base and Sei, where SeiPay shared Sei's grand prize. Social and culture winners (T3) were rare and mostly came from partner or category prizes. Security, risk and agent infrastructure (T4) took a steady share of places but rarely the top prize; the exception was Hyperliquid Korea, where the Edgescan security scanner placed 2nd.

## Q2: Which winners used real-time or low-latency execution?

### Takeaway
The clearest latency-dependent winners were HyperFlash (millisecond arbitrage), Hyper Homo (real-time copy trading), Hyperliquid Copilot (real-time signals plus execution), Somnia's BALL3 (real-time onchain PvP) and Somnia's Reactivity set, where contracts fire on events without keepers. MegaETH's showcase real-time apps came from its accelerator rather than a hackathon: stream-based prediction markets and live game shows.

### Cited Findings
- HyperFlash is a "cross-chain arbitrage engine" that executes trades in milliseconds without locking up capital (HLH 6th). Hyper Homo mirrors top trader positions in real time (4th). Copilot offers real-time signals and automated execution (1st) — [WuBlockchain](https://wublock.substack.com/p/hyperliquid-korea-hackathon-wrap).
- Spread won RedStone's prize as an NBBO router, a best-execution tool that depends on fresh price data — same.
- BALL3 brings Agar.io mechanics fully onchain as real-time PvP. Somnia's Dreamathon required teams to build a "real-time web3 app" in GameFi, DeFi, prediction markets, SocialFi or InfoFi over 8 weeks — [Somnia blog](https://blog.somnia.network/p/dreamathons-first-class-of-founders); [Invezz](https://invezz.com/news/2025/08/28/somnia-launches-200k-dreamathon-incubator-driving-new-opportunities-of-decentralized-social-and-information-finance/).
- The Somnia Reactivity hackathon drew over 350 developers and 146 projects. Its pitch was that validators "invoke contracts directly when triggers fire" with no polling or keepers. Featured projects included copy trading with chained subscriptions (Mirra), real-time risk monitoring (ReactGuard) and auto-payout insurance (SomniaSure) — [Somnia blog](https://blog.somnia.network/p/somnia-reactivity-hackathon-shows).
- MegaETH's real-time apps: Sweep (stream-based prediction market), Pump Party (interactive live game shows) and Hunch (fast-resolving micro-trend markets). These came from the Mega Mafia accelerator, not a hackathon. MegaETH now says it will focus on "OMEGA" apps, meaning apps that can only run on MegaETH — [CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/megaethkills-mega-mafia-after-backed-apps-desert-the-network); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2026-04-23-megaeth-first-kpi-10-apps-live-seven-day-tge-countdown-398651).
- Several Mega Mafia teams left MegaETH. HelloTrade moved to Monad, Noise launched on Base, and GTE built its own appchain — [CryptoDaily](https://cryptodaily.co.uk/2026/07/megaeth-ends-mega-mafia-after-migrations).
- Monad's Gorillionaire delivers "real-time Buy/Sell signals" — [Envio](https://docs.envio.dev/blog/announcing-the-monad-envio-hackathon-winners).
- I found no Sei order-book hackathon winners in any source.

### Inferences
- Judges reward latency when the demo makes it visible: live PvP, live copy trading, millisecond arbitrage, fast-resolving markets. A claim of "fast" alone does not win.
- MegaETH's "only possible here" test (OMEGA apps) is a useful filter for Monad Metropolis pitches: explain why the app would break on a 2-second chain.

### Gaps
- I found no latency benchmarks or judge commentary tying a specific win to speed.

## Q3: Published judging criteria and what organizers said they wanted

### Takeaway
Criteria were rarely published in detail. Where they were, they stressed a working product, real-world usefulness, UX and growth potential (Base), or "weird, powerful, boundary-pushing" agents (Moltiverse). Incubator-style events (Somnia Dreamathon, Base Batches) scored teams on sustained progress across milestones and investor-readiness.

### Cited Findings
- **Base Onchain Summer 2024 criteria:** technicality (does it work), creativity, real-world utility, aesthetics/UI-UX, and future growth. Extra pools went to projects using Smart Wallet, OnchainKit, Verify and Paymaster (40 ETH), and 5 ETH went to community quadratic-voting picks — [Base blog](https://blog.base.org/announcing-the-onchain-summer-buildathon-winners); [Devfolio](https://devfolio.co/blog/onchain-summer-buildathon-recap/).
- **Moltiverse (Monad):** organizers wanted agents that are "weird, powerful, and push boundaries". Judges were Haseeb (Dragonfly), Frankie (Paradigm), Cookies (Monad), LILMON (nad.fun) and Patrick Chu (AUSD). Bounties covered a Gaming Arena Agent, a World Model Agent and a Religious Persuasion Agent. Judging was rolling, which favored early submitters — [moltiverse.dev](https://moltiverse.dev/). Suggested ideas included agent-to-agent transactions, wagering and game agents, world creation and robotics — [Monad on X](https://x.com/monad/status/2018710997716894162).
- **Somnia Dreamathon:** teams were scored on consistent progress across checkpoints, iteration on feedback, and working prototypes, with Supafund's AI-driven milestone scoring — [Somnia blog](https://blog.somnia.network/p/dreamathons-first-class-of-founders).
- **Sei ai/accelathon:** each track was judged on its own criteria, with one winner and one runner-up per track. Track winners pitched to a VC panel — [Sei blog](https://blog.sei.io/introducing-the-ai-accelathon/).
- **Sonic DeFAI:** the brief asked for AI agents that take both social and onchain actions. No criteria were disclosed in the winners post — [DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/external/sonic-labs-announces-250000-sonic-defai-hackathon-in-partnership-with-dorahacks-and-zerebro/); [Sonic Labs](https://www.soniclabs.com/blog/winners-announced-sonic-defai-hackathon/).
- **Hyperliquid:** the stated mission was growing the ecosystem to "house all of finance". Tracks were Public Goods and Frontier — [Paragraph](https://paragraph.com/@hl-hackathon/hyperliquid-community-hackathon).
- **Monad Blitz:** winners are chosen by participant or audience vote, not judges (for example ETHDenver 2026 and Pune V2) — [Luma ETHDenver](https://luma.com/monad-blitz-ethdenver-2026); [Luma Pune V2](https://luma.com/blitz-pune-july-2026).
- **Base Batches 003:** the program targeted DeFi, AI and prediction markets, with 2-minute investor pitches — [Base blog](https://blog.base.org/introducing-base-batches-003-2).

### Inferences
- Across these events, the repeated signals are a working demo, a clear user, use of the sponsor's native primitives (for example Base's Smart Wallet and Paymaster, Somnia's Reactivity, Hyperliquid's HIP-3), and investor-readiness in accelerator formats.

### Gaps
- I found no published scoring rubrics or weightings for Sonic, Hyperliquid or Sei.

## Q4: Portable ideas (won elsewhere; no Monad equivalent confirmed) and overdone ideas

### Takeaway
Several winning ideas have no hackathon-winning equivalent on Monad that I could confirm:
- decentralized trader chat embedded in trading frontends (Hyperchat)
- real-time social copy trading (Hyper Homo, Mirra)
- NBBO best-execution routers (Spread)
- delta-neutral basis vaults (BASIS X)
- oracle-free margin trading for long-tail assets (LIKWID)
- universal QR merchant payments with onchain loyalty (DPay)
- fully onchain real-time PvP (BALL3)
- creator coins for musicians (Carbon Opus)
- event-driven automation such as auto-inheritance and parametric insurance (SomMemo, SomniaSure)
- no-code workflow builders for onchain automation (Streamline)

Overdone: generic "AI trading copilot/signals" bots, AI yield optimizers, "agent + token" launches, and Telegram trading bots.

### Cited Findings
- AI trading and portfolio agents won on every chain I checked:

  - Monad: Gorillionaire, plus KiSignals by name — [Envio](https://docs.envio.dev/blog/announcing-the-monad-envio-hackathon-winners)
- Agent-plus-token pairing appears in Sonic's Memegents and HyperSonic, and was a required Moltiverse track ($140K) — [Sonic Labs](https://www.soniclabs.com/blog/winners-announced-sonic-defai-hackathon/); [moltiverse.dev](https://moltiverse.dev/).

### Inferences
- **Portable ideas for Monad Metropolis, by track:**
