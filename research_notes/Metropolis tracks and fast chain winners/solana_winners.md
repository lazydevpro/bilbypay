# Solana (Colosseum) Hackathon Winners 2024–2026, Judging Patterns, and Mapping to Monad Metropolis Tracks

Scope: Renaissance (Mar–Apr 2024), Radar (Sep–Oct 2024), Breakout (Apr–May 2025), Cypherpunk (Sep–Oct 2025), Frontier (Apr–May 2026). Monad Metropolis tracks: (1) Onchain Finance & Trading, (2) Consumer Products & Payments, (3) Social, Attention & Culture, (4) Trust, Identity & AI Infrastructure. Tags like [T1] map each winner to a track.

## Q1. Grand champions and category winners (name, what it built, what made it win)

### Takeaway
Grand champions are almost always an unexpected *new primitive* rather than a polished app (Ore = PoW mining on Solana; Reflect = hedge-backed stablecoin; TapeDrive = cheap onchain storage; Unruggable = hardware wallet; CrowdBrain = robotics-teleop DePIN). Track winners skew toward DeFi/stablecoins/payments and consumer betting/prediction/trading. By 2025–26 the center of gravity moved hard to stablecoin payments, x402/agent payments, prediction markets, RWAs and TCG/collectibles.

## Q2. Which winners exploited speed / low fees specifically?

### Takeaway
The clearest speed-native winners are real-time games (Supersize, sub-30ms via MagicBlock ephemeral rollups), trading/market-structure apps (Urani, Archer batch auctions, Squeeze, Blackpool, The Arena, Rekt), high-frequency micro-markets (Pregame, Fanplay hamster racing, Mentioned, Trepa), mining/proof loops (Ore, TapeDrive miners solving challenges every minute), and agent micropayments (Latinum, MCPay, Corbits, Flovia, Clawpump).

### Cited Findings
- Supersize: "sending transactions every 30 milliseconds. They're landing every 50 milliseconds"; uses MagicBlock Ephemeral Rollups for sub-30ms PvP; MVP on devnet at hackathon start, mainnet around hackathon end. Windfall (3rd, Radar gaming) also built on MagicBlock. — [MagicBlock blog](https://www.magicblock.xyz/blog/supersize-hackathon); [Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/news/solana-gaming-startup-open-sources-validator)
- TapeDrive miners "solve data storage challenges in parallel every minute"; pitch is cost (1,400x cheaper storage). — [Blockworks](https://blockworks.com/news/solana-data-startup-wins-hackathon)
- Ore: PoW mining where anyone can mine onchain (inherently high tx volume / cheap tx dependent). — [Colosseum blog](https://blog.colosseum.com/announcing-the-winners-of-the-solana-renaissance-hackathon/)
- Latinum: agents autonomously pay for services via HTTP 402 payments (micropayments). — [NDRC](https://www.ndrc.ie/blog/latinum-i-see-a-world-in-eighteen-months-where-an-ai-agent-handles-all-everyday-online-transactions-for-you); MCPay connects MCP and x402; Corbits is an x402 merchant dashboard — [Colosseum Cypherpunk blog](https://blog.colosseum.com/announcing-the-winners-of-the-solana-cypherpunk-hackathon/)
- Archer — "trading engine utilizing dual flow batch auctions"; Urani — intent-based anti-MEV swap aggregator; High TPS Solana Client — tx-capacity optimizations. — [Cypherpunk blog](https://blog.colosseum.com/announcing-the-winners-of-the-solana-cypherpunk-hackathon/); [Renaissance blog](https://blog.colosseum.com/announcing-the-winners-of-the-solana-renaissance-hackathon/)
- Superteam Canada guide advises "build something that's only possible on Solana" (i.e., lean on speed/cost). — [GitHub guide](https://github.com/SuperteamCanada/how-to-win-colosseum-hackathon)

### Inferences
- Speed is rarely the headline pitch; it is the enabler behind a consumer loop (betting on micro-events, live PvP, group trading) or agent-payment flow. Winners pitch the user/business outcome and justify the chain in the technical demo.
- Micro-event markets (hamster racing, mention markets, sentiment prediction) are a repeated winning pattern that only works with sub-second, sub-cent settlement — direct fit for Monad (400ms blocks).

### Gaps
- No Colosseum source quantifies latency/fee usage for most winners beyond Supersize and TapeDrive.

## Q3. Colosseum's published judging advice and common reasons projects lose

### Takeaway
Colosseum judges like early-stage investors: they reward full-time teams with a viable business, founder-market fit, a working devnet demo with an "aha moment", and a crisp <3 min pitch video. Projects lose to friction (broken/localhost demos, no access to materials), buzzwords without a hypothesis, unclear market, and treating the hackathon as the finish line.

### Cited Findings
- "Prizes will be awarded to teams who intend to build full-time and develop products with potentially viable business models" (public-good award excepted). — [How to Win a Colosseum Hackathon](https://blog.colosseum.com/how-to-win-a-colosseum-hackathon/)
- Average winning team size is above 3, mixing technical and non-technical skills; judges value founder-market fit / domain expertise; prioritize features that create an amazing working demo with an "aha moment" on devnet. — [Colosseum blog](https://blog.colosseum.com/how-to-win-a-colosseum-hackathon/)
- Process: Colosseum staff run multiple rounds to shortlist; judging panel scores; a smaller group gets a 15-minute Zoom interview; weekly updates optional but recommended. No set bounties; must integrate Solana in some capacity; 5-week format. — [Colosseum hackathon page / how-to-win summary](https://colosseum.com/hackathon); [How to win](https://blog.colosseum.com/how-to-win-a-colosseum-hackathon/)
- Pitch video (<3 min) is usually the first thing judges review and determines shortlisting; should cover team background, problem, who it's for. Separate 2–3 min technical demo should explain implementation and how you leverage Solana — "avoid treating it as a second pitch". — [Perfecting Your Hackathon Submission](https://blog.colosseum.com/perfecting-your-hackathon-submission/)
- Judges want evidence of real user problems / traction: "early traction, conversations with potential users, feedback on platforms like Twitter or Telegram." — [Colosseum workshop recap](https://blog.colosseum.com/perfecting-your-hackathon-submission/)
- Mistakes listed: unpolished/incomplete videos; not explaining chain integration; buzzwords instead of a concrete hypothesis; leaving optional fields blank; treating hackathon as an end point; not granting judges access to docs/videos/repos. — [Colosseum workshop recap](https://blog.colosseum.com/perfecting-your-hackathon-submission/)
- Community guidance: judges spend ~3–5 min/project; if they can't understand it in 60 seconds you lose; localhost demos fail; keep scope small (one new swap mechanism with great UX, not a full DEX). — [Superteam Canada guide](https://github.com/SuperteamCanada/how-to-win-colosseum-hackathon). A two-time winner/judge: "The more friction you add, the more likely it is you will get ignored" — make it work, usable, appealing, in that order; airdrop devnet funds for judges. — [Superteam Japan playbook](https://superteam-japan.gitbook.io/hackathon-playbook)
- Persistence: "Developers often enter multiple hackathons before being selected as a winner" (Unruggable entered 4 before winning). — [How to win](https://blog.colosseum.com/how-to-win-a-colosseum-hackathon/); [Cypherpunk blog](https://blog.colosseum.com/announcing-the-winners-of-the-solana-cypherpunk-hackathon/)
- Accelerator selection criteria (Cohort 5): "technical talent, speed, vision, and competitive drive required to build successful, enduring products." — [Cohort 5 post](https://blog.colosseum.com/announcing-colosseums-accelerator-cohort-5/)

### Inferences
- For Metropolis: lead with a 3-min founder/problem/user video, a zero-friction hosted demo with pre-funded testnet wallets, and a separate technical video explaining why Monad (parallel EVM, 400ms blocks) specifically matters.

### Gaps
- Colosseum does not publish a numeric scoring rubric; "common reasons projects lose" are inferred from their mistake lists, not a post-mortem dataset.

## Q5. Portable winning ideas for Monad (EVM fast chain), mapped to Metropolis tracks

### Takeaway
Monad already has order-book/perps DEXs (Kuru, Perpl, Hanji), early prediction/sports betting apps (Kizzy, Levr Bet, RareBet) and a Foundation x402 data platform — so plain CLOBs, generic sports betting, or basic x402 gateways are crowded. Less-covered, portable winner patterns: micro-event/mention markets, prediction-market aggregators and structured products, group/social trading, agent-managed thematic portfolios, BNPL/credit, stablecoin FX, revenue tokenization, TCG collectibles economies, real-time onchain PvP games, and agent payment analytics/security.

- Monad mainnet Nov 24, 2025; Kuru (CLOB+AMM, $11.6M Paradigm-led), Perpl (perps), Hanji (orderbook, #2 weekly volume Jan 2026), Drake Exchange, MondayTrade; Aave V3 live Jul 2, 2026; 150+ apps in 8 months; TVL ~$988M incl. $774M stablecoins. — [PANews](https://www.panewslab.com/en/articles/e8fdbff9-3288-48be-bf2e-ac8bfebb3972); [Backpack Learn](https://learn.backpack.exchange/articles/monad-ecosystem); [CoinMarketCap AI](https://coinmarketcap.com/cmc-ai/monad/latest-updates/) (aggregator; treat figures cautiously)

- Early Monad prediction apps: Kizzy Mobile (social-metric bets), Levr Bet (leveraged sports), RareBet Sports. — [PANews](https://www.panewslab.com/en/articles/e8fdbff9-3288-48be-bf2e-ac8bfebb3972)

### Inferences (port candidates; "not on Monad" status NOT exhaustively verified)
- **T1 Onchain Finance & Trading:** Capitola-style prediction-market meta-aggregator (route across Polymarket/Kalshi/Monad markets); Senthos-style structured products on prediction exposure; Archer-style batch-auction engine as MEV-resistant alternative to CLOBs; Exponent-style yield tokenization/yield order book (Pendle exists on EVM; check Monad deployment); Dropset/FXSwap onchain FX for stablecoin pairs; attn.markets revenue tokenization; Peaks/Cesto agent-managed thematic baskets using Kuru/Perpl liquidity.
- **T2 Consumer Products & Payments:** Yumi-style onchain BNPL with underwriting; Credible/KinnectFi diaspora remittance neobank; Zoneless open-source "Stripe Connect for USDC"; Sp3nd-style pay-with-stablecoins-for-Amazon; WeLikeSports no-loss fantasy sports funded by yield on pooled deposits (Aave now on Monad).
- **T3 Social, Attention & Culture:** Mentioned-style mention markets and Fanplay/Trepa micro-event markets that exploit 400ms blocks; Alpha Group Trading / Fora group-chat trading; Banger-style tweet/creator trading; Supersize-style real-time onchain PvP (Monad's parallel EVM could do this without an ephemeral rollup — a strong "only possible here" demo); TCG collectibles economy (One Arena/Traded.gg/JK Index); Superfan meta-record label.
- **T4 Trust, Identity & AI Infrastructure:** MCPay/Latinum MCP<->x402 payment middleware for agent tool calls (differentiate from Monad's data-only x402 platform); Flovia agent-payment analytics; Sudont agent firewall/transaction security; Seer-style tx debugger for parallel-EVM; Autonom RWA oracle; Humanship ID privacy human identity; Trenches.top reputation tokenization for alpha callers.
- Highest-leverage pattern for Metropolis: pick a winner archetype whose core loop needs sub-second, sub-cent settlement (micro-markets, live PvP, agent pay-per-call), then pitch it as a company with a real user segment — matching Colosseum-style judging.
