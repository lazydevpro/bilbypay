# Passkey mobile apps unlock Metropolis's richest stacks

A solo developer's best odds in Monad Metropolis come from combining one main-track entry with sponsor bounties whose requirements overlap. The strongest combination is a **mobile app that uses Mera passkey login and holds an AUSD balance**. It can compete for Agora's two **$10,000** bounties (payments in Track 2, trading in Track 1). The same build also fits Mera's **$2,500** UX bounty, and add-ons from Perpl, Envio, Nansen or Aurora. Each of the four tracks pays **$10,000 each to 1st, 2nd and 3rd place**. Bounties stack on top of the main-track prize ([Prizes](https://hackathon.monad.xyz/prizes)). All four tracks use the same rubric, and it rewards startup potential more than the tech: **Founder & Market Readiness is weighted 25%** and Traction another 20% ([Track 01](https://hackathon.monad.xyz/tracks/onchain-finance)). That matches what fast-chain hackathons have rewarded. Colosseum's grand champions were new primitives with a fundable business behind them. Hyperliquid, Sonic and Monad's own events kept awarding trading tools and agents that move money. Generic "AI trading copilots" usually finished in honorable-mention slots. The ideas that won elsewhere and have no Monad equivalent yet are copy-trading, micro-event "mention" markets, stablecoin remittance, user-owned agent memory and TCG economies. Each of them lines up with a suggested starting point on the Metropolis platform. With **19 days left** before the Oct 13, 11:59 PM ET deadline (Oct 14, 09:29 IST on the platform clock), check four conflicts before building: two published rubrics, open source "encouraged" versus "mandatory", a $145K guaranteed pool against a "$250K+" headline, and whether Monad has a P256 precompile.

## One investor-weighted rubric judges all four tracks

The platform's rule is to "Choose one primary track, then add as many sponsor bounties as you'd like," and it adds that "A team of one is a team" ([Tracks](https://hackathon.monad.xyz/tracks)). Each track page is built the same way: an "About" essay, a core question, a "Belongs here if" test, a weighted rubric, and a numbered list of suggested starting points. **The weights are the same everywhere**: Technical Execution 20%, Design & Craft 20%, Originality & Track Insight 15%, Founder & Market Readiness 25%, and Traction & Path Forward 20%. The Social page says it is "applied consistently across all four tracks" ([Track 03](https://hackathon.monad.xyz/tracks/social-culture)). What changes from track to track is how each criterion is worded. Track 1 asks for "a specific first user beyond 'crypto traders'". Track 2 tells judges to "judge harshly on any point of friction that reveals 'this is crypto'". Track 3 asks whether the onchain layer is "decorative". Track 4 counts developer experience as design and treats "even one other team integrating it during the hackathon" as traction ([Track 02](https://hackathon.monad.xyz/tracks/consumer-payments); [Track 04](https://hackathon.monad.xyz/tracks/trust-identity-ai)).

| Track | Core question (verbatim, condensed) | "Belongs here if" | What the rubric specifically punishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **01 Onchain Finance & Trading** | "Where are the missing asset primitives — and what new trading experiences…become possible when settlement is fast enough to stop being the bottleneck?" | Core output is "a financial instrument, market, asset primitive, or trading experience"; user is a trader, protocol or product builder | "a UI mockup over static data"; "a faster clone of an existing DeFi product" |
| **02 Consumer Products & Payments** | "What does a financial product look like when onchain rails are leveraged as an advantage to design?" | Primary user is a consumer "who may not identify as a crypto user"; value is "a financial experience, not a trading or market-making product" | Any friction that reveals crypto; "a wallet/payments app with new branding"; "everyone needs payments" |
| **03 Social, Attention & Culture** | How to make "social participation, cultural engagement, and attention translate into real ownership and economic stake" | "Core user value is social connection, cultural participation, or community — even if financial mechanics are involved" | "a token-gated feature bolted onto a generic interface"; onchain layer that is "decorative" |
| **04 Trust, Identity & AI Infra** | "What does the trust and data ownership layer look like for an AI-native internet" | "Primary output is a protocol, primitive, or infrastructure layer that other applications build on — not a standalone consumer product" | Incorrect WebAuthn/P256 use, leaked secrets; designs that "centralize the problem differently" |

Sources: the four track pages linked above. The marketing page adds one headline example set per track, which are also good pitch hooks. **Track 1**: "Fully onchain order books that do not need an offchain matching engine," "Perpetuals with funding that updates every block," and "Undercollateralised lending priced on onchain credit history". **Track 2**: "A payments app that never mentions a blockchain," "Subscriptions that charge by the second," and "Shared wallets and group spending". **Track 3**: "A feed where curation is paid for by the people who benefit from it," "Ticketing and access that follows a person," and "Markets on cultural outcomes rather than financial ones". **Track 4**: "Passkey-native accounts using P256 and WebAuthn," "Agent identity and reputation under ERC-8004," and "Provenance for generated media that survives re-encoding" ([Metropolis](https://www.monad.xyz/developers/hackathons/metropolis)). The marketing page also says who each track is for. Track 1 is for "Teams who have shipped a trading, lending, or market-making product before". Track 3 is for "Teams who have grown a community, not only built one". Track 4 is for teams "comfortable with cryptography, protocol design, or agent frameworks". Track 2's description, "Product teams who care more about a user's first five minutes than the architecture underneath", is the only one that doesn't ask for prior experience or an existing community. That makes it the easiest fit for a solo developer.

Every track page lists the same six deliverables ([Track 01](https://hackathon.monad.xyz/tracks/onchain-finance)):

| Deliverable | Requirement |

Judging runs Oct 14–27 and winners are announced **Nov 3**. A separate **$25,000 Grand Champion** prize is chosen "across all four tracks," and top teams are invited to a residency and to DeltaV ([Metropolis](https://www.monad.xyz/developers/hackathons/metropolis)). There are two panels. The main-track panel is mostly investors: Electric, Nansen, Castle Island, Paradigm, Dragonfly, Pantera, Galaxy, CoinFund, IOSG and others, plus Keone Hon and Eunice Giarta of the Monad Foundation. Bounties are judged by a separate sponsor panel ([Prizes](https://hackathon.monad.xyz/prizes)).

The amounts are uneven across tracks. By my count, the cash bounties total **$76,500**, and another **$11,000** is paid in cloud or AI credits. Bounties tagged to Track 1 are worth **$25,500**. Track 2 has one $10,000 bounty. Track 3 has only $2,000, and that is in Tencent credits. Track 4 has $2,000 in cash plus $5,000 in Qwen credits. The "All tracks" bounties add about **$39,000 in cash**, but $5,000 of that needs a campus community a solo builder won't have. On top of this, sponsors contribute in-kind services to winners worth roughly $79K at headline value: ack3 security scans, Chainstack, Crouton, Mercuryo, Zerion, Envio hosting, Spectrum, and a PingBusiness merchant rebate of up to $20K ([Prizes](https://hackathon.monad.xyz/prizes)). Adding **$145K** in main prizes, $76.5K in cash bounties, credits and in-kind services gives about the "$250K+" in the marketing headline. That explains the prize-pool conflict below. It also means that about 40% of the headline number isn't cash.

Stacking has one real conflict: the account layer. Agora's two bounties and Mera's UX bounty all need Mera to be the authentication layer. Privy and Dynamic each want their own SDK doing more than login. One participant treats Dynamic and Mera as mutually exclusive; that is their inference, not a rule. So a builder has to pick a side. The Mera side is worth more ($10K + $2.5K) than either Privy or Dynamic ($5K each). The participant-quoted rules also weight bounty judging **40% on "Adherence to the published bounty requirements"**, which means meeting each checklist item exactly matters more than being clever ([participant repo](https://github.com/precious-akpan/monad-metropolis-merchant-rails)).

## Fast-chain winners had a sharp hook, not a generic AI copilot

Colosseum's grand champions were each a new primitive with a business behind it. Ore added proof-of-work mining; Reflect, a hedge-backed stablecoin that later raised a **$3.75M** seed led by a16z CSX; TapeDrive, storage it claimed was 1,400x cheaper; Unruggable, a hardware wallet; and CrowdBrain, robotics-teleop DePIN ([Colosseum Frontier](https://blog.colosseum.com/announcing-the-winners-of-the-solana-frontier-hackathon/); [Blockworks](https://blockworks.com/news/reflect-stablecoin-infra-raise)). The themes moved from DePIN and MEV-protected swaps in 2024, to stablecoin payments, privacy and x402 agent payments in 2025, to prediction and "opportunity" markets, agent portfolios, TCG economies and diaspora neobanks in 2026. Frontier drew **2,857 projects**. Colosseum dropped ranked placements for it and published a flat list of 25 winners it considered investable, and only **0.7%** of submissions made its accelerator ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/colosseum-announces-26-winners-of-the-solana-frontier-hackathon-the-largest-crypto-hackathon-ever); [Cohort 5](https://blog.colosseum.com/announcing-colosseums-accelerator-cohort-5/)). Colosseum's advice to entrants is the same as Metropolis's 25% founder weighting. Prizes go "to teams who intend to build full-time," and judges look for a working demo with an "aha moment." The listed mistakes are buzzwords without a hypothesis, not explaining the chain integration, and blocking judges' access to the materials ([How to win](https://blog.colosseum.com/how-to-win-a-colosseum-hackathon/); [Submission guide](https://blog.colosseum.com/perfecting-your-hackathon-submission/)). Community judges add that each project gets about 3–5 minutes of attention, so demos running on localhost lose ([Superteam Canada](https://github.com/SuperteamCanada/how-to-win-colosseum-hackathon)).

Trading-focused chains show a different pattern. At Sonic DeFAI, Shōgun, an AI yield allocator, won **$60K** and Nuvolari, a portfolio manager, won $55K. Most other Sonic prizes went to AI trading chat agents ([Sonic Labs](https://www.soniclabs.com/blog/winners-announced-sonic-defai-hackathon/)). At Hyperliquid Korea, an AI trading copilot took 1st with 12,000 USDC. Second place, however, went to **Edgescan**, a security scanner. The rest of the top 10 were copy-trading (Hyper Homo), millisecond arbitrage (HyperFlash), a delta-neutral basis vault (BASIS X) and risk scoring ([WuBlockchain](https://wublock.substack.com/p/hyperliquid-korea-hackathon-wrap)). Somnia's Reactivity event rewarded products whose contracts fire on events without keepers: copy-trading (Mirra), auto-payout crop insurance (SomniaSure), auto-inheritance (SomMemo) and real-time risk monitoring (ReactGuard). Its Dreamathon picked BALL3, a fully onchain real-time PvP game ([Somnia Reactivity](https://blog.somnia.network/p/somnia-reactivity-hackathon-shows); [Somnia Dreamathon](https://blog.somnia.network/p/dreamathons-first-class-of-founders)). Sei split its **$75K grand prize** between SeiPay, which handles agentic payments, and Kurtosis, which designs "Quantum Markets" ([Sei](https://blog.sei.io/ecosystem/the-roundup171125/)). Base rewarded payments and commerce such as Slice's gasless checkout inside Farcaster Frames, and its 2024 criteria named aesthetics, UI/UX and "future growth" explicitly ([Base](https://blog.base.org/announcing-the-onchain-summer-buildathon-winners)). **MegaETH ran no hackathon with published winners**. Its real-time showcase apps (Sweep, Pump Party, Hunch) came out of the Mega Mafia accelerator, which it closed in July 2026 after teams left. One of those teams, HelloTrade, moved to Monad ([CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/megaethkills-mega-mafia-after-backed-apps-desert-the-network); [CryptoDaily](https://cryptodaily.co.uk/2026/07/megaeth-ends-mega-mafia-after-migrations)).

Monad's own events have gone the same way. The evm/accathon winners were Gorillionaire, an AI signals terminal that also won Envio's prize, plus KiSignals, StageFun, OwnPay and StitchAI, an agent memory layer ([Envio](https://docs.envio.dev/blog/announcing-the-monad-envio-hackathon-winners)). Rebel in Paradise went to OpenAlice, a trading agent, with TickPay, which streams micro-payments, also among the winners ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/monad-ai-hackathon-concludes-with-11-winning-projects-and-major-llm-partnerships)). Moltiverse paid **16 winners $10K each** from 400+ entries. Its judges included Frankie from Paradigm, who is also on the Metropolis panel ([moltiverse.dev](https://moltiverse.dev/); [Monad on X](https://x.com/monad/status/2026363272249655540)).

| User-owned agent memory and agent-payment analytics | StitchAI (Monad), Flovia, MCPay (Solana) | **Portable** to Track 4 with a Mera PRF angle |

One caveat: "no Monad equivalent" means only that I found none among Monad's published hackathon winners. It is not a product-level check of Monad mainnet, and the full Moltiverse winner list was not available.

## Five builds that give a solo developer the best odds

The shortlist below scores each option on four things: whether a track-tagged bounty with few competitors exists, whether the requirements of the stacked bounties overlap, whether some version of the idea has already won somewhere, and whether one person can ship a mobile or web demo in 19 days that works on a fresh device. The "Ceiling" column is the most the build could win if every prize it stacks paid out at the maximum. It is not an expected value.

| # | Build | Track | Bounties it can stack | Ceiling (cash) | Winner precedent |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Passkey remittance app**: a mobile AUSD "send home" app with Mera onboarding, a named corridor (e.g., US→India or Gulf→South Asia), instant settlement, and funds arriving from another chain via Aurora | 02 | Agora Payments $10K, Mera UX $2.5K, Aurora Intents up to $2.5K, Envio $1K (+ Alchemy credits) | ~$26K | Credible, DashX, KinnectFi, Nedapay |
| 2 | **Social copy-trading on Perpl, mobile**: Mera login, AUSD balance, follow a leader wallet ranked with Nansen smart-money data, and mirror their trades through a risk-capped automation | 01 | Agora Trading $10K, Perpl API $5K, Nansen $5K, Mera UX $2.5K, Envio $1K | ~$33.5K | Hyper Homo, Mirra; "open lane" per Metropolis Resources |
| 3 | **Mention markets on Kuru**: event contracts on whether a phrase is said in an earnings call, stream or keynote, resolved by a Chainlink CRE workflow that checks the transcript with an LLM | 01 | Kuru New Markets $5K, Chainlink CRE $3K, Envio $1K, Kimi credits | ~$19K + credits | Mentioned, Memetic Machines, Trepa |
| 4 | **Passkey-encrypted cross-app AI memory**: a user-owned context vault whose keys come from Mera PRF, with agents holding ERC-8004 identities and paying per read | 04 | Mera Many Keys $2.5K, Qwen $5K credits, Kimi $3K credits, Envio $1K | ~$13.5K + credits | StitchAI (Monad), Wootz |
| 5 | **Early-supporter registry for creators**: index fans' first tips or holdings, issue verifiable early-fan proofs, and route a share of creator revenue back to them automatically | 03 | Nansen $5K, Envio $1K, Privy or Dynamic $5K, Hunyuan credits | ~$21K + credits | Banger.lol, Nomu, Carbon Opus |

**Build 1** is my top pick for a solo developer. Track 2 is the one track that asks about a user's first five minutes rather than prior shipping experience or an existing community. Its rubric rewards exactly what a polished mobile remittance flow shows: a named consumer segment, no visible crypto, and settlement fast enough to feel instant. Agora's bounty requires mobile, Mera and AUSD, so only teams willing to build that exact stack compete for it. The corridor also answers the 25% founder criterion with a specific first user. A test with "five friends" meets Track 2's traction bar. The risk is that payments apps are the most visibly contested category, so polish and the stateless-login demo have to carry it.

**Build 2** has the highest ceiling and uses the Resources page's own statement that copy-trading is "an open lane" on Monad. The design has to fit two bounties at once. Perpl's API bounty scores "good risk management" and real onchain activity, and Agora's trading bounty scores how well Mera, AUSD and Perpl work together. The overdone-AI trap is easy to avoid by leaving an LLM out entirely. The risk is regulatory and trust framing. Track 1's Design criterion asks for "clear risk disclosure," so position caps and a visible kill switch have to appear in the demo.

**Build 3** gives the most "only possible on Monad" story. Markets that resolve within seconds of a spoken word depend on sub-second blocks and near-zero fees. The idea is a close match for a Frontier winner and for the Resources idea "Prediction Markets for Unpriced Outcomes." Kuru's bounty, however, asks for "legal/operational viability" and a liquidity strategy, and prediction markets are a crowded category overall. **Build 4** is the best fit for Track 4. Mera's bounty lists "passkey-encrypted AI agent memory" as an example, and Track 4 lists cross-app AI memory as a starting point. Most of its stack is credits, though, and Track 4 counts "one other team integrating it" as traction, which is hard to arrange alone. **Build 5** is a hedge for Track 3, the track with almost no tagged bounty money and so probably less bounty-driven competition. Its weakness is that Track 3 judges ask whether a community "would migrate," which a solo developer with no community will struggle to answer.

## Conclusion

Metropolis's written materials make it a startup competition. Of the rubric weight, 45% goes to founder insight and traction, while only 20% goes to technical execution. The main-track judges are mostly investors. For a solo developer, the problem to solve is therefore not "which idea is most novel" but "which idea lets one person show a named user, a working mobile flow, and several bounty checklists met at once." Agora and Mera have written almost the same stack (mobile, passkey, AUSD) into three bounties worth $22.5K between them. That is the clearest signal of what Monad's partners want and the least crowded path to stacking. Colosseum's trajectory shows that the grand prize is a small part of the value, and the same may hold here: Monad's residency, DeltaV and post-event funding likely matter more than the $10K. That argues for a build you would keep working on after Nov 3.

This research also changes one recommendation from the earlier strategy work. Track 3 has the Foundation's clearest idea requests, such as markets on cultural outcomes. But it carries almost no bounty money and asks for teams that "have grown a community." Once bounties are counted, Track 2 with the Agora remittance stack, or Track 1 with Perpl copy-trading, gives a solo developer better expected value. The first day of work should go to confirming P256 on the target network and reading the gated Rules modal directly. Those two checks would settle the remaining unverified assumptions in this report.
