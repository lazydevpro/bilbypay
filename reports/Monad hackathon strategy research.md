# Win Metropolis by building where Monad is thin

Monad has a lot of money on chain but few real users, and its hackathons reward projects that could change that. Past Monad winners mostly fell into two groups. The first is consumer products with a clear user loop, such as sports betting, social apps, ad rewards and events crowdfunding. The second, especially since late 2025, is AI agents that handle money, such as trading agents and agent-to-agent payments. Pure infrastructure rarely took first place. The chain now presents itself as "high-frequency finance" for institutions. Its TVL is about **$1.01B**, but only about **12–22K daily active addresses** use it, and analysts name the missing native consumer "killer app" as its main weakness. The Foundation has published its wishlist in the four Metropolis tracks. It wants payments that never mention a blockchain, subscriptions charged by the second, shared wallets, fully onchain order books, perps with funding that updates every block, markets on cultural outcomes, and passkey accounts built on the P256 precompile. The rules as quoted by participants give **"Monad Integration" 20% of the score** and require an explanation of why the project needs Monad, so a generic EVM app will lose points. The best fit for a solo builder is a narrow consumer product that visibly depends on 300ms blocks, 600ms finality and near-zero fees. Specifically, that means a cultural-outcome or self-resolving market (Track 3), or a passkey-native payments app (Track 2). Launchpads, liquid staking tokens (LSTs), generic DEXs and perp exchanges, lending markets and plain ERC-8004 agent-trust layers are already crowded. As of today (Sept 24) there are **19 days left** before the Oct 13, 11:59 PM ET deadline, so scope matters as much as the idea.

## Past winners paired a consumer loop or an AI agent with money

Monad has run four kinds of builder events since 2024. The first is the **Monad Madness** VC pitch series (NYC, Seoul, Bangkok, Hong Kong), which admits only teams that already have an MVP ([Monad](https://www.monad.xyz/announcements/monads-1m-pitch-competition-monad-madness)). The second is the flagship online hackathons: **evm/accathon** (Feb–Mar 2025, $100K), **Moltiverse** (Feb 2026, $200K, AI agents) and now **Metropolis** ([Monad Substack](https://monadxyz.substack.com/p/the-first-monad-hackathon-evmaccathon); [moltiverse.dev](https://moltiverse.dev/)). The third is regional events such as **Rebel in Paradise**, an AI hackathon in China ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/monad-ai-hackathon-concludes-with-11-winning-projects-and-major-llm-partnerships)). The fourth is **Monad Blitz**, one-day local events judged by peer vote, with 50+ editions so far ([Luma](https://luma.com/monad-blitz-ethdenver-2026)). The themes shifted over time. The 2024–25 events favored consumer apps, gaming and DeFi. The 2026 events are dominated by AI agents.

The winners follow the same arc. At Madness NYC, first place went to **EarnOS**, a web3 ads and rewards platform with email login and chain abstraction. Second was **Sauce.GG**, a memecoin trading platform, and third was **LEVR**, which combines leveraged investing with sports ([Gate Learn](https://www.gate.com/learn/articles/monad-madness-nyc-startup-competition-winners-announced-six-innovative-projects-showcased/4376)). The Bangkok cohort included **RareBetSports** (daily fantasy sports and betting paid in USDC), **Kizzy** (a social app linking users with creators) and **Sparkball**, a game ([Bitget/BlockBeats](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560604366660)). The evm/accathon winners included **KiSignals**, a sentiment-aggregation terminal, **StageFun**, which crowdfunds events, **OwnPay**, which does RFID payments, **Gorillionaire**, an AI trading terminal built on Envio indexing that also won Envio's sponsor track, and **StitchAI**, a memory layer for agents ([Nadsletter #4](https://medium.com/@monadwhisper/nadsletter-issue-4-monad-updates-as-of-april-2025-17a31f89a2b1); [Envio](https://docs.envio.dev/blog/announcing-the-monad-envio-hackathon-winners)). In 2026, **OpenAlice** won Rebel in Paradise. It is a trading agent that keeps transparent logs. Other winners there were **TickPay** (high-frequency micro-streaming payments), **Agora-mesh** (agent service discovery with onchain settlement) and **A2A IntentPool** (settlement of tasks between machines) ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/monad-ai-hackathon-concludes-with-11-winning-projects-and-major-llm-partnerships)). Moltiverse drew **400+ entries** and named 16 winners, described as "agent VCs, dating apps, open worlds" ([Monad on X](https://x.com/monad/status/2026363272249655540)).

Across all of these, top prizes went to products with a user loop, or to agents that move money quickly. None were standalone developer tools. Moltiverse is the only event with published criteria. They were agent functionality and innovation, Monad integration, technical substance, and community traction, which Moltiverse measured with a market-cap prize ([moltiverse.dev](https://moltiverse.dev/)). The Metropolis judges lean heavily toward investors: Keone Hon and Eunice Giarta of the Foundation, plus Galaxy, Electric Capital, Nansen, Paradigm, CoinFund, IOSG and Castle Island ([Metropolis](https://monad.xyz/developers/hackathons/metropolis)). The post-event funding is also larger than the prizes. evm/accathon offered up to **$30K in milestone funding** to every participating team, and the Nitro accelerator gave **up to $500K per team** ([Monad Substack](https://monadxyz.substack.com/p/the-first-monad-hackathon-evmaccathon); [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/nitro-backs-execution-first-monad-startups-with-up-to-500k-each/)). Given that panel, a working product that looks fundable should score better than novelty alone. This is an inference: I found no first-person statement from Monad leadership about what they look for in submissions.

### Metropolis rules and scoring as currently known

Metropolis runs **Sept 1 – Oct 13, 2026**. Judging is Oct 14–27 and winners are announced **Nov 3**. The official page lists **$30K per track** (split across 3 teams), a **$25K Grand Champion** and sponsor bounties, and the whole event runs on mainnet (chain ID 143) ([Metropolis](https://monad.xyz/developers/hackathons/metropolis)). The official rules sit behind a login. Participants have quoted them in public repos, and those quotes give **five criteria at 20% each**: Product Quality & Completeness, Technical Excellence, Monad Integration, Track Fit & Problem Relevance, and Innovation & Impact. According to the same quotes, a submission needs:

- an OSI open-source license
- a demo video of **3 minutes or less** that shows real Monad transactions ("not mockups or slides")
- contract addresses or transaction hashes
- a section explaining why the project needs Monad's capabilities (§9.2)
- a disclosure in the README of any AI tools used

The quotes also say teams can have 1–5 people and each project enters exactly one track ([participant repo](https://github.com/precious-akpan/monad-metropolis-merchant-rails)). Two points conflict and should be checked on hackathon.monad.xyz. The quoted rules put the prize total at **$145K** in cash, not the "$250K+" in the marketing. They also appear to require open source, while the public page only encourages it.

## Monad sells "high-frequency finance" while users stay scarce

Monad's pitch has moved from developer-facing technology to financial infrastructure. From 2022 to 2024, Keone Hon described a performant, decentralized EVM that runs on consumer hardware ([The Pipeline](https://medium.com/@pipeline.xyz/why-monad-super-optimizing-the-evm-with-ceo-keone-hon-e3b9cb38b6de)). In late 2024 his theme was the cost of trading: "in DeFi right now, people are very used to paying 30 basis points or 50 basis points or 1%" ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2024/12/10/keone-hon-bridging-de-fi-and-trad-fi)). By 2026 the Foundation called Monad **"The Home of High-Frequency Finance."** That positioning covers onchain spot and perps trading, FX, private credit, stablecoin payments and tokenized assets ([Monad blog](https://monad.xyz/blog/monad-home-high-frequency-finance)). The homepage now reads "Enabling Open Access to Financial Markets" and shows logos from Mastercard, Circle, MetaMask, Aave and Coinbase ([monad.xyz](https://monad.xyz/)).

The biggest story of summer 2026 is the **MetaMask Money Account**. It is a self-custodial USD account that pays yield on mUSD and supports card spending, and it runs only on Monad. MetaMask pays all the gas. It reportedly chose Monad because sub-second finality is fast enough for card authorization ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/372298/crypto-wallet-metamask-yield-paying-money-accounts-monad)). In the same period the Foundation joined the **x402 Foundation**, which works on agent payments, and it now runs its own x402 facilitator ([Monad blog](https://blog.monad.xyz/blog/monad-foundation-joins-x402-foundation); [Monad docs](https://docs.monad.xyz/guides/x402)). Consumer crypto still appears in the messaging, but mostly as onboarding tools like MetaMask and passkeys rather than as a headline vertical.

Mainnet and the MON token launched on **Nov 24, 2025** ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2025/11/24/monad-blockchain-goes-live-with-100b-token-supply-and-airdrop)). By the capital numbers, the ecosystem is doing well:

- **TVL** was about $1.01B on Sept 24, 2026, close to its all-time high of $1.04B. It was $200–450M through the first half of 2026 ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/chain/monad)).
- **Stablecoins** total about **$698M** ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/chain/monad)).
- **Spot DEX volume** set a monthly record of **$3.7B** in August 2026 ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/chain/monad)).

Most of the growth is in institutional-style DeFi. Aave V3 holds $291M, Euler $262M and Morpho $194M, curator vaults such as K3 add more, and Valos's RWA credit product holds $106M. **Kuru's order book (CLOB)** handles most spot volume, about $2.66B over 30 days, but its TVL is only $1.25M ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/chain/monad)).

Usage looks much weaker. Daily active addresses fell about **85%**, from roughly 117K right after launch to 12–22K ([BlockEden](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/04/18/monad-vs-megaeth-high-performance-evm-mainnet-battle-2026/)). Chain gas fees are about **$431K per 30 days** against $1B of TVL ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/chain/monad)). AMBCrypto said the TVL record came while "demand hasn't caught up to the rally" ([AMBCrypto](https://ambcrypto.com/monad-hits-new-tvl-high-at-868m-but-demand-hasnt-caught-up-to-the-rally/)). The consumer and meme apps of 2025 have largely faded. Nad.fun's monthly fees fell from about $810K to $30–100K, and Kizzy now shows about $0.07M in TVL ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/nad.fun)). Analysts describe both Monad and its rival MegaETH as "mostly populated by ports of Ethereum incumbents" and expect developers to follow whichever chain first builds a standout native app ([BlockEden](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/04/18/monad-vs-megaeth-high-performance-evm-mainnet-battle-2026/)). The token adds pressure. MON trades around **$0.024**, about half its peak, and the first team and investor unlock arrives **Nov 24, 2026**, three weeks after winners are announced ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/monad); [Tokenomist](https://tokenomist.ai/monad)).

This affects which projects will stand out. Monad already has plenty of DeFi capital. What it lacks is retained users and a product that could only exist on Monad. A project that plausibly brings non-crypto users onto the chain speaks to Monad's most visible weakness. A lending fork adds to something it already has too much of.

## The Foundation has published a specific wishlist

Monad has no formal "Request for Startups" document, but the Foundation has stated clearly what it wants. The most direct statement is the Metropolis track list, where each track names concrete example ideas ([Metropolis](https://monad.xyz/developers/hackathons/metropolis)):

| Track | Foundation pitch | Named example builds |
|---|---|---|
| 01 Onchain Finance & Trading | "Fast settlement makes new financial instruments and fully onchain markets more practical" | Onchain order books without an offchain matching engine; perps with funding that updates every block; undercollateralized lending priced on onchain credit history |
| 02 Consumer Products & Payments | "Instant, programmable, and invisible to the end user" | "A payments app that never mentions a blockchain"; subscriptions charged by the second; shared or group wallets that settle up without an intermediary |
| 03 Social, Attention & Culture | Open social graphs, programmable incentives, fast settlement | Feeds where curation is paid for by the people who benefit; ticketing that follows the person; "Markets on cultural outcomes rather than financial ones" |
| 04 Trust, Identity & AI Infra | Identity, provenance, data ownership and agent trust are "urgent" | Passkey-native accounts using P256/WebAuthn with no seed phrase; ERC-8004 agent identity; provenance for generated media |

According to the quoted rules, Track 2 is narrower than its title suggests. The primary user must be a consumer "who may not identify as a crypto user," and the core value must be "a financial experience rather than a trading or market making product" ([participant repo](https://github.com/precious-akpan/monad-metropolis-merchant-rails)). A trading app does not qualify for Track 2.

Several other published sources point the same way:

- **Monad AI Blueprint (Nov 2025).** It asks for agentic commerce "where AI agents pay each other on crypto rails," reputation layers for agents, generative AI with provenance and monetization onchain, and verifiable agent memory ([Monad](https://www.monad.xyz/announcements/introducing-monad-ai-blueprint)).
- **Self-resolving prediction markets (Nov 2025 Foundation post).** These are markets on subjective outcomes that resolve by final consensus. The post ends: "We'd love to see teams prototype it on crypto-native rails!" ([Monad blog](https://www.monad.xyz/blog/self-resolving-prediction-markets)). This is one of the few direct requests from Foundation staff, and it matches Track 3's "markets on cultural outcomes."
- **Onboarding and agent tooling.** The docs include an x402 payment guide and an ERC-8004 registration tutorial, and in July the Foundation shipped **"mera,"** a passkey onboarding library ([Monad blog](https://blog.monad.xyz/blog/monad-foundation-joins-x402-foundation); [Monad news](https://monad.xyz/blog/tags/news)).

The sponsor bounties show which incumbents want integrations. Reported bounties include Kuru ("best trading app on the Kuru orderbook"), AUSD ("best mobile trading app"), MetaMask ("best agent wallet plugin"), and prizes from Alibaba Cloud/Qwen and Kimi. Sponsors also include Chainlink, Privy, Dynamic, Envio, Nansen and Agora ([Rise In](https://www.risein.com/monad/monad-metropolis-hackathon); [Metropolis](https://monad.xyz/developers/hackathons/metropolis)). Integrating one of these can win a bounty on top of a track prize.

## Monad's real technical edges: speed, fees and precompiles

Monad is a bytecode-compatible EVM L1. Its consensus layer orders transactions without executing them, and execution runs afterward (asynchronous execution). Execution is optimistically parallel but commits in serial order, so results match Ethereum exactly and developers need no access lists ([Monad docs](https://docs.monad.xyz/monad-arch/execution/parallel-execution.md)). Other parts of the stack are a custom trie-native database (MonadDb), a JIT that compiles frequently used contracts to native code, erasure-coded block propagation (RaptorCast), and **MonadBFT**, which gives speculative finality in one round and full finality in two ([How Monad Works](https://monad.xyz/blog/how-monad-works); [MonadBFT docs](https://docs.monad.xyz/monad-arch/consensus/monad-bft.md)). Developers mostly don't see any of this. The capabilities an app can use are these:

- **300ms blocks and 600ms finality** since MIP-12 on July 23, 2026. Many explainers still quote the older 400/800ms figures ([Monad blog](https://monad.xyz/blog/300ms-block-times)).
- **Fees of about $0.0005 for a 200k-gas transaction** at the minimum base fee ([Gas Pricing](https://docs.monad.xyz/developer-essentials/gas-pricing.md)). Perpl reportedly handles about **1,000 order posts and cancels per second at about $0.0001 each** ([Monad blog](https://monad.xyz/blog/monad-home-high-frequency-finance)).
- **A 128 KB contract size limit**, against 24 KB on Ethereum. A complete matching engine can fit in one contract.
- **P256VERIFY (EIP-7951) at address 0x0100**, for verifying passkey and WebAuthn signatures onchain, plus BLS12-381 precompiles ([Precompiles](https://docs.monad.xyz/developer-essentials/precompiles.md)).
- **Commit-state WebSocket feeds**. `monadNewHeads` and `monadLogs` report each block as Proposed, then Voted, then Finalized. A UI can show a result optimistically at about 300ms and mark it settled at about 600ms ([JSON-RPC docs](https://docs.monad.xyz/reference/json-rpc/overview.md)).
- **Storage pages (MIP-8, live Sept 2, 2026).** Storage is now warmed per 128-slot page, so later reads within the same page cost 100 gas. Data stored together is up to 98% cheaper to access ([Releases](https://docs.monad.xyz/developer-essentials/changelog/releases); [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/monads-mainnet-upgrade-makes-storing-data-together-98-cheaper/)).

"10,000 TPS" is design capacity, not observed load. Early mainnet averaged tens of transactions per second and peaked around 350 ([Monad blog](https://monad.xyz/blog/contextualizing-blockchain-performance); [Monad Block](https://monadblock.com/2026/02/10/monad-mainnet-performance-140m-transactions-10k-tps-and-220m-defi-tvl-breakdown/)). A pitch should argue from latency, finality and cost per action, not TPS. Compared with competitors, Monad's niche is a decentralized EVM L1 with 200+ validators. **MegaETH** is faster, with 10ms blocks, but relies on a single sequencer. **Solana** is not EVM. **Hyperliquid** is built only for perps. **Base** has far more distribution but slower finality ([BlockEden](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/04/18/monad-vs-megaeth-high-performance-evm-mainnet-battle-2026/); [Monad blog](https://monad.xyz/blog/300ms-block-times)). A one-line version for a pitch: "Solana-class latency, Ethereum tooling, L1 decentralization."

### Gotchas that break Ethereum assumptions

| Gotcha | What happens | Design response |
|---|---|---|
| You pay for the **gas limit**, not gas used | Deducted amount is `value + gas_bid × gas_limit` ([Differences](https://docs.monad.xyz/developer-essentials/differences)) | Use `eth_estimateGas` plus about a 7.5% buffer ([Category Labs](https://www.category.xyz/blogs/setting-your-gas-limit-on-monad)). Never hardcode 1M-gas limits in bots or agents |
| **10 MON reserve balance** per EOA | A transaction that dips below the reserve can be included and then revert, and it still pays gas. EIP-7702-delegated EOAs can never go below 10 MON and cannot use CREATE/CREATE2 ([Reserve Balance](https://docs.monad.xyz/developer-essentials/reserve-balance.md)) | Fund demo wallets and session keys with more than 10 MON. At about $0.024 per MON that is roughly $0.24 per user, so it is affordable to sponsor |
| **New accounts wait about 3 blocks** before sending | Consumer apps whose first action is "fund, then act" | Add about 1s of buffer or sponsor gas through a paymaster |
| **Cold access costs 10,100 gas** (about 4x Ethereum); crypto precompiles cost 2–5x more ([Opcode Pricing](https://docs.monad.xyz/developer-essentials/opcode-pricing.md)) | Designs that touch many contracts or use many signatures in one transaction | Keep calls in few contracts and pack state into structs or arrays to benefit from page warming |
| **No global mempool**; `eth_getTransactionByHash` returns null while a transaction is pending | Mempool watchers and pending-transaction UIs | Use the `monadNewHeads`/`monadLogs` commit-state feeds instead |
| **Historical state kept for only about 40K blocks** (hours at 300ms) ([Historical Data](https://docs.monad.xyz/developer-essentials/historical-data.md)) | `eth_call` against old block numbers | Index events with Envio (a sponsor) or a similar indexer |
| **Hot storage slots serialize** under optimistic parallel execution | Single global pools or counters | Shard state per user or per market. This is inferred from how optimistic concurrency works; the docs don't quantify the penalty |
| Public RPCs are limited to **15–25 requests/second** ([Network Info](https://docs.monad.xyz/developer-essentials/network-information/index.md)) | Demo-day load | Use a sponsor RPC such as Alchemy or QuickNode |

## What to build: Track 3 first, then Track 2, and leave the crowded markets alone

The evidence points to one approach: pick a consumer-facing product that the Foundation has literally named, and make Monad's latency and cost visible in the demo. The table below ranks realistic solo projects that fit in 19 days.

| Rank | Idea | Track | Why it fits | Competition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Real-time cultural-outcome or self-resolving market.** Examples: live markets on streams, awards shows or sports moments that settle in 600ms; or a subjective market that resolves by final consensus | 03 | Requested twice, in the Track 3 list and in a Foundation blog post. Past winners include sports betting and social-betting apps. Commit-state feeds allow instant "bet placed / bet settled" UX | Lowest visible competition among public repos (weak evidence). Generic sports and prediction apps already exist (LEVR, Kizzy, RareBet), so the cultural or self-resolving angle is what sets it apart |
| 2 | **Passkey-native payments app that never mentions a blockchain.** P256 at 0x0100, EIP-7702 or mera, and USDC or mUSD, used for per-second subscriptions or a group wallet that splits and settles bills | 02 | Matches the Foundation's flagship MetaMask/payments story. Each named Track 2 example depends on sub-cent fees and sub-second finality. Could also pick up MetaMask or Privy bounties | Visibly contested: public repos already cover merchant invoicing, family remittance and similar. Win on polish and invisibility, not on the idea |
| 3 | **Paid-curation feed or ticketing that follows the person** | 03 | Named Track 3 examples with a clear consumer loop | Few visible competitors, but "Monad Integration" is harder to argue unless the design uses per-action micropayments |
| 4 | **Consumer or mobile trading front end on Kuru's order book**, or a small perp with funding that updates every block | 01 | Uses the flagship "high-frequency finance" story and can win the Kuru and AUSD bounties | Building a new perp or DEX is crowded (Perpl, LeverUp, Monday Trade, Kuru, Hanji). Building on top of Kuru avoids that |
| 5 | **Passkey account and agent-trust layer**, such as ERC-8004 identity plus x402 payments, or provenance for generated media | 04 | Strong alignment with the AI Blueprint, x402 and ERC-8004 | Most contested: ÆGIS, AgentWitness and others are visible. Stand out only with provenance for generated media or a consumer-facing passkey angle |

The following categories are crowded and should be avoided as standalone projects: memecoin launchpads (Nad.fun, Bonad, Printr, Clanker and others), liquid staking (ShMonad, Kintsu, Magma, aPriori), perp DEXs and spot DEXs or aggregators, generic lending markets (Aave, Euler, Morpho, Curvance), and generic prediction markets ([Atomic Wallet](https://atomicwallet.io/academy/articles/monad-day-1-guide); [CryptoRank](https://cryptorank.io/ecosystems/monad); [Gate Learn](https://www.gate.com/learn/articles/monad-ecosystem-guide-consumer-d-app-native-prediction-market/6036)). A fork of any of these would score poorly on Innovation & Impact and give the judges nothing new.

The evidence has limits. Competition estimates come from searching public GitHub repos. Many entrants will stay private until the deadline, and hundreds of projects is a plausible total given Moltiverse's 400+. The scoring weights come from a participant's quote of the rules, not from the official platform. It is also possible that Track 4 receives the most attention from judges because AI is the dominant 2026 theme. The counter-argument is that the visible competition there is densest, and past AI winners were agents that moved money, not trust infrastructure alone.

## Conclusion

The main opportunity comes from a mismatch: Monad has institutional capital but lacks retained, native users, and the Metropolis tracks are close to a written request for the missing consumer layer. A solo builder does best by treating the "Monad Integration" criterion (20%) and the §9.2 "why Monad" section as the design brief. Choose a feature that fails on Ethereum L1 and is noticeably worse on a typical L2, such as a bet that settles in 600ms, a subscription charged by the second, or a passkey signature verified onchain, and put it in the first 30 seconds of the 3-minute demo. Two operational choices also improve the odds. First, integrate a sponsor (Kuru, Privy, MetaMask, Envio) so the project can win a bounty as well as a track prize. Second, design around the gas-limit and reserve-balance gotchas from day one, because a demo transaction that is included but reverts is the easiest way to lose points on Product Quality.

Two decisions remain open and should be settled before building. The first is whether cash prizes total $145K or $250K+, which changes the expected value for each track. The second is whether open source is required, which the gated official rules would settle. Winners are announced on Nov 3, three weeks before MON's first large unlock. A project that produces a credible "real users on Monad" story then is also well placed for the Foundation's post-hackathon funding (residency invitations, Nitro-style accelerators, milestone grants), which has historically been worth more than the hackathon prizes.
